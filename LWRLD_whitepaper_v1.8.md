## 4. Tokenomics

### 4.1 Supply

Max supply is one billion LWRLD. On Flap, a new token starts on a bonding curve. Most of the supply sits in the curve until people buy it. When the project reaches Flap's graduation threshold, liquidity is designed to migrate to a DEX pool, commonly PancakeSwap on BNB Chain. Until then, the bonding curve is the market.

There is no team, VC, or advisor allocation and no vesting table. Supply is issued through the launchpad, not a private presale. If that ever changes, this paper will be revised and the change will be dated.

A 9 September 2026 snapshot showed about 5.005 million circulating and the rest still in the curve, with one early buyer holding most of that small float. That was concentration of the float, not a hidden claim on the other supply. Those counts are old. Read the live numbers on Flap.

An early buyer took the first tokens. The 4% tax did not stop that. It only made a fast flip more expensive.

### 4.2 The 4% tax

Every buy and every sell on the taxed pool pays 4%. That 4% is split on-chain:

| Bucket | Share of the 4% | Share of the trade | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | 85% | 3.40% | Goes back into the pool |
| Community / project fund | 10% | 0.40% | Project wallet for building, not a silent payday |
| Burn | 5% | 0.20% | Taken out of supply over time |

Worked example. Someone buys $100 of LWRLD on the taxed pool:

- $4.00 is tax.
- $3.40 of that tax is routed toward liquidity.
- $0.40 is routed toward the fund wallet.
- $0.20 is routed toward burn.
- The trader receives roughly $96 of LWRLD at the then-current price, before any other wallet or pool fees.

The fund recipient is:

`0x859B0D91376A6bBF28B1F12F68E916e59708092F`

### 4.3 Why 4%

The tax was chosen to make sniping and dump-and-run expensive. A buy and a sell is about an 8% round trip before slippage. It raises the cost of flipping. It does not stop someone from buying first.

### 4.4 What the tax is not

The fund slice can help pay for streams, art, hosting, or the next feature. It is not a treasury-backed protocol. The burn is not a price guarantee. The tax is not a dividend. Holding $LWRLD does not pay you BNB, and it does not pay you Bitcoin.

This is the design for a working system. It is not built. There is no second contract, no set percent, and no Bitcoin held.

$LVRSE is functional only when every piece below is true at the same time, and a new version of this paper names them. Until then it is a plan.

1. **One contract, published first.** The ticker is $LVRSE. Chain and address go in this paper before any sale. Until that address exists, there is no $LVRSE. A different address is not it.

2. **A funding rule, written before launch.** Half of profits are swapped to Bitcoin. What counts as profit, which wallet swaps, and the transaction are public before anyone is asked to treat it as done. None has been swapped. The other half is not assigned.

3. **A holder record.** A snapshot of contract `0x3899ebf76c768c156df3073031a4518c5f947777` at a block number published in advance. Every wallet holding $LWRLD at that block is in the record. The rule for buys after that block is written before the snapshot, not after.

4. **A percent, set in public first.** The percent is one number, published before the snapshot, and the same rule for every wallet in that record. Today that number is not set. No contract pays it. A promise with no number and no contract is not a payment.

5. **A claim the holder starts.** The holder claims from their own wallet. The site never asks for a seed phrase. There is no surprise send. If the claim contract is not deployed, nobody has been paid. The share gives. It does not take from the holder's $LWRLD balance.

6. **Bitcoin, before anyone says it decided anything.** Bitcoin decides the fate of $LWRLD and $LVRSE only after three things are public: the BTC address, a balance anyone can check, and a written rule for what that balance does. No Bitcoin is held. The fate is undecided. Neither coin is redeemable for BTC until that rule is published and the BTC is actually there.

7. **Proof on one page.** The BTC address, the BTC balance, and $LVRSE's supply, side by side. If any of those are missing, nothing has been decided.

8. **The switch.** It counts as a functional system only when all of these exist together: the published $LVRSE contract, the published percent, the snapshot block, the claim path, the public BTC balance, the written fate rule, and this paper updated with those facts. One of them is not enough.

What this system will not do: promise a price, pay a yield on $LWRLD, redeem $LWRLD for BTC, or use a second contract that was not written here first.
